PART 1 — RESEARCH (retrieved provisions)
────────────────────────────────────────

1. What makes the engagement "casual"
Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A (snapshot 2026-07-07). An employee is a casual employee "only if" —
  (1)(a) "the employment relationship is characterised by an absence of a firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work"; and
  (1)(b) "the employee would be entitled to a casual loading or a specific rate of pay for casual employees under the terms of a fair work instrument ... or the employee is entitled to such a loading or rate of pay under the contract of employment."
Assessment is made "on the basis of the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the employment relationship" (s 15A(2)(a)), and a firm advance commitment can arise from "a mutual understanding or expectation ... not rising to the level of a term of that contract" (s 15A(2)(b)). So contract wording alone does not settle status — conduct does.
s 15A(5): a casual employee "remains a casual employee" until one of the listed events occurs.

2. Casual loading and what it substitutes for
Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 545A (snapshot 2026-07-07). Where an employer pays "an identifiable amount (the loading amount) paid to compensate the person for not having one or more relevant entitlements", and the person was in fact not a casual employee, a court "must reduce (but not below nil)" any claim amount by the loading amount. Under s 545A(3)(a) the court has regard to a term that "specifies the relevant entitlements the loading amount is compensating for and specifies the proportion of the loading amount attributable to each such entitlement". Relevant entitlements are listed in s 545A(4): paid annual leave; paid personal/carer's leave; paid compassionate leave; payment for absence on a public holiday; payment in lieu of notice of termination; redundancy pay.
Consequence for drafting: an itemised loading clause is what s 545A(3)(a) refers to. Clause 5.2 currently does not itemise proportions — see OPEN ITEMS 12.

3. Leave position of casuals
s 96(1): paid personal/carer's leave accrues "For each year of service with an employer (other than periods of employment as a casual employee of the employer)".
s 106: paid compassionate leave applies to "an employee, other than a casual employee"; Note: "For casual employees, compassionate leave is unpaid leave."
s 106A(2)(c): paid family and domestic violence leave "is available in full to part-time and casual employees" — 10 days per 12 month period (s 106A(1)), paid for a casual "at the employee's full rate of pay, worked out as if the employee had worked the hours in the period for which the employee was rostered" (s 106BA(1)(b)).

4. Mandatory statements
s 125B(1): "An employer must give a casual employee the Casual Employment Information Statement: (a) before, or as soon as practicable after, the employee starts employment as a casual employee with the employer; and (b) as soon as practicable after the employee has been employed ... for a period of 6 months ...; and (c) as soon as practicable after [12 months, and each subsequent 12 months]." Paragraph (1)(b) and subparagraph (1)(c)(ii) "do not apply if ... the employer is a small business employer" (s 125B(2)).
s 125(1): the Fair Work Information Statement must be given to "each employee before, or as soon as practicable after, the employee starts employment."

5. Employee choice about casual employment (what replaced conversion)
s 66AAB: "A casual employee may give an employer a written notification" where the employee believes they no longer meet s 15A(1)–(4), subject to service of "at least 12 months" (small business employer) or "at least 6 months" (other employers).
s 66AAC(1): the employer "must give an employee a written response ... within 21 days"; s 66AAC(3) requires consultation first; s 66AAC(4) lists the only grounds for not accepting.

6. Superannuation
s 116B: "An employer must make contributions to a superannuation fund for the benefit of an employee so as to avoid liability to pay superannuation guarantee charge under the Superannuation Guarantee Charge Act 1992 in relation to the employee."

7. Notice of termination
s 117(4): "A reference in this section to continuous service with the employer does not include periods of employment as a casual employee of the employer." The s 117 notice table therefore does not build up on casual service. Any contractual notice period is a separate matter — see OPEN ITEMS 17.

8. Minimum standards floor / misuse risks
s 61(1): the NES "sets minimum standards that apply to the employment of employees which cannot be displaced"; s 61(2)(ba) includes "casual employment (Division 4A)". s 44: "An employer must not contravene a provision of the National Employment Standards."
s 359B: an employer "must not dismiss, or threaten to dismiss" an employee "in order to engage the individual as a casual employee to perform the same, or substantially the same, work" (civil remedy provision).
s 359C: prohibits a knowingly false statement made "in order to persuade or influence the individual to enter into a contract for casual employment" (civil remedy provision).
