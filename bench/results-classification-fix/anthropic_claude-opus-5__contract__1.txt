RESEARCH — provisions engaged

1. Meaning of casual employee. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A (snapshot 2026-07-07). An employee is a casual employee "only if: (a) the employment relationship is characterised by an absence of a firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work; and (b) the employee would be entitled to a casual loading or a specific rate of pay for casual employees under the terms of a fair work instrument if the employee were a casual employee, or the employee is entitled to such a loading or rate of pay under the contract of employment."

2. Substance over label. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A(2) (snapshot 2026-07-07). The absence of a firm advance commitment is assessed "on the basis of the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the employment relationship". A firm advance commitment can arise "in the form of a mutual understanding or expectation between the employer and employee not rising to the level of a term of that contract". A regular pattern of work is one consideration under s 15A(2)(c)(iv), and the note states: "A regular pattern of work does not of itself indicate a firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work."

3. Status continues until a specified event. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A(5) (snapshot 2026-07-07). A person who starts as a casual employee "remains a casual employee of the employer until" one of the listed events occurs, including a change of status under Division 4A of Part 2-2 or an FWC order under s 66MA or s 739.

4. Casual Employment Information Statement. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 125B(1) (snapshot 2026-07-07). "An employer must give a casual employee the Casual Employment Information Statement: (a) before, or as soon as practicable after, the employee starts employment as a casual employee with the employer; and (b) as soon as practicable after the employee has been employed by the employer for a period of 6 months beginning the day the employment started". Paragraph (1)(b) does not apply to a small business employer: s 125B(2).

5. Employee choice about casual employment. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 66A(1) (snapshot 2026-07-07): "This Division applies in relation to an employee who is a casual employee." The object in s 66AAA is a framework that "provides for the resolution of disputes to support employee choice about employment status".

6. Casual loading and later claims. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 545A(3)(a) (snapshot 2026-07-07). A court has regard to a contract term that "specifies the relevant entitlements the loading amount is compensating for and specifies the proportion of the loading amount attributable to each such entitlement". The relevant entitlements are listed in s 545A(4): paid annual leave, paid personal/carer's leave, paid compassionate leave, payment for absence on a public holiday, payment in lieu of notice of termination, and redundancy pay.

7. Prohibited conduct. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 359B (snapshot 2026-07-07): an employer "must not dismiss, or threaten to dismiss" an employee "in order to engage the individual as a casual employee to perform the same, or substantially the same, work". Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 359C (snapshot 2026-07-07) prohibits a statement the employer "knows is false" made "in order to persuade or influence the individual to enter into a contract for casual employment".

This draft treats the employee as a national-system employee. The Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) and the applicable Modern Award govern the employment. No WA state employment legislation is cited.
