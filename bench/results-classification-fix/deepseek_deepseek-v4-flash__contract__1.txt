## RESEARCH - Casual employment under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)

**Definition of casual employee**

Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A (snapshot 2026-07-07) states:

An employee is a casual employee only if:

- the employment relationship is characterised by an absence of a firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work; and
- the employee would be entitled to a casual loading or a specific rate of pay for casual employees under the terms of a fair work instrument, or is entitled to such a loading under the contract of employment.

Assessment is made on the basis of the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the relationship. Indicative factors include whether the employer can elect to offer work and the employee can elect to accept or reject it, whether continuing work is reasonably likely, and whether there is a regular pattern of work.

**Casual Employment Information Statement**

Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 125B (snapshot 2026-07-07) requires an employer to give a casual employee the Casual Employment Information Statement:

- before or as soon as practicable after the employee starts casual employment; and
- after 6 months of employment; and
- after 12 months of employment and after each subsequent 12-month period.

**Misrepresentation and dismissal protections**

Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 359C (snapshot 2026-07-07) prohibits an employer from making a false statement to persuade an individual to enter into casual employment to perform the same work they previously performed other than as a casual.

Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 359B (snapshot 2026-07-07) prohibits an employer from dismissing an employee to engage them as a casual employee to perform the same work.

**Casual loading set-off**

Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 545A (snapshot 2026-07-07) provides that if a person was described as casual and paid a loading amount, but was not a casual employee, a court must reduce any claim amount for relevant entitlements (paid annual leave, personal/carer's leave, compassionate leave, public holiday pay, payment in lieu of notice, redundancy pay) by the loading amount.

**Casual conversion**

Division 4A of Part 2-2 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) (snapshot 2026-07-07) sets out the employee choice and employer obligation framework for casual conversion. Transitional provisions are in Schedule 1 clauses 44-47A and clause 102.
