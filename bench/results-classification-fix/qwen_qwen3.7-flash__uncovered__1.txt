CLASSIFICATION: RESEARCH - domain: privacy

Under the *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth), Part IIIC establishes the scheme for notification of eligible data breaches. The key sections are 26WE, 26WF, 26WK, 26WL, 26WR, and 26WD. The snapshot date for these provisions is 4 June 2026.

---

### What constitutes an eligible data breach (s 26WE)

An eligible data breach occurs when:

- An APP entity, credit reporting body, credit provider, or file number recipient holds personal information relating to one or more individuals; and
- There is unauthorised access to, or unauthorised disclosure of, the information; and
- A reasonable person would conclude that the access or disclosure would be likely to result in serious harm to any of the individuals to whom the information relates; or
- The information is lost in circumstances where unauthorised access to, or unauthorised disclosure of, the information is likely to occur, and assuming such access or disclosure were to occur, a reasonable person would conclude that it would be likely to result in serious harm to any of those individuals.

---

### Remedial action exception (s 26WF)

The breach is not treated as an eligible data breach if the entity takes remedial action before the access or disclosure results in serious harm, and as a result, a reasonable person would conclude that serious harm is unlikely. This operates as an exception so that no notification is required.

---

### When notification is triggered (s 26WA and 26WK)

A breach triggers notification obligations if either of two conditions is met:

1. The entity has reasonable grounds to believe that an eligible data breach has happened; or
2. The Commissioner directs the entity to give notification.

If an entity becomes aware of reasonable grounds to believe there has been an eligible data breach, s 26WK(2) requires the entity to do both of the following "as soon as practicable after the entity becomes so aware":

(a) prepare a statement that complies with subsection 26WK(3); and
(b) give a copy of the statement to the Commissioner.

The statement must set out (s 26WK(3)):

(a) the identity and contact details of the entity; and
(b) a description of the eligible data breach that the entity has reasonable grounds to believe has happened; and
(c) the particular kind or kinds of information concerned; and
(d) recommendations about the steps that individuals should take in response to the eligible data breach that the entity has reasonable grounds to believe has happened.

---

### Notification to affected individuals (s 26WL)

After preparing the statement, the entity must, as soon as practicable, notify the affected individuals using one of three methods (in ranked order):

(a) If practicable — take reasonable steps to notify each individual to whom the relevant information relates of the contents of the statement; or
(b) Otherwise, if practicable — take reasonable steps to notify each individual who is at risk from the eligible data breach; or
(c) If neither (a) nor (b) is practicable — publish a copy of the statement on the entity's website (if any) and take reasonable steps to publicise the contents of the statement.

If the entity normally communicates with an individual using a particular method, the notification may use that method (s 26WL(4)).

---

### Direction by the Commissioner (s 26WR)

If the Commissioner is aware of reasonable grounds to believe an eligible data breach has occurred, the Commissioner may direct the entity by written notice to:

(a) prepare a compliant statement and give it to the Commissioner; and
(b) notify affected individuals using the same three-tier method described above (individual notification, or website publication + publicisation).

Before giving a direction, the Commissioner must invite the entity to make a submission. The entity must comply with a direction as soon as practicable after it is given.

The Commissioner's statement must include:

(a) the identity and contact details of the entity;
(b) a description of the eligible data breach;
(c) the particular kind or kinds of information concerned; and
(d) recommendations about steps individuals should take.

---

### Exception — My Health Records notification (s 26WD)

If the same access, disclosure, or loss has been, or is required to be, notified under section 75 of the *My Health Records Act 2012*, this Part does not apply in relation to that access, disclosure or loss.

---

### Breach as interference with privacy (s 13(4A))

If an entity contravenes s 26WH(2), s 26WK(2), s 26WL(3), or s 26WR(10), the contravention is taken to be an act that is an interference with the privacy of an individual.
