2. NATURE OF EMPLOYMENT

   2.1 The Employee is engaged as a casual employee in the position of
   [MISSING: position title], commencing on [MISSING: commencement date].

   2.2 There is no firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work.
   Each engagement is a separate period of work. The Employer offers it. The
   Employee accepts it or declines it.

   2.3 The Employee does not receive the paid leave entitlements that accrue to
   full-time and part-time employees, except as provided by the Fair Work Act
   2009 (Cth), the National Employment Standards and [MISSING: modern award
   name].

   Clause 2.2 and clause 2.3 are based on Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A
   (snapshot 2026-07-07). Section 15A(1) provides that an employee is a casual
   employee "only if: (a) the employment relationship is characterised by an
   absence of a firm advance commitment to continuing and indefinite work; and
   (b) the employee would be entitled to a casual loading or a specific rate of
   pay for casual employees under the terms of a fair work instrument if the
   employee were a casual employee, or the employee is entitled to such a
   loading or rate of pay under the contract of employment."

   Section 15A(2) provides that the absence of a firm advance commitment is
   assessed "on the basis of the real substance, practical reality and true
   nature of the employment relationship". A contract term alone does not
   settle the status.

   Clause 5.3 is based on Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 545A (snapshot

   "specifies the relevant entitlements the loading amount is compensating for

   (d) payment for absence on a public holiday; (e) payment in lieu of notice of

6. CASUAL EMPLOYMENT INFORMATION STATEMENT

   6.1 The Employer gives the Employee the Casual Employment Information
   Statement before the Employee starts work, or as soon as practicable after
   the Employee starts work, and again at the times required by law.

   Clause 6.1 is based on Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 125B (snapshot
   2026-07-07). Section 125B(1) provides that an employer must give a casual
   employee the Statement "(a) before, or as soon as practicable after, the
   employee starts employment as a casual employee with the employer; and (b) as
   soon as practicable after the employee has been employed by the employer for
   a period of 6 months beginning the day the employment started; and (c) as
   soon as practicable after the following: (i) the employee has been employed
   by the employer for a period of 12 months beginning the day the employment
   started; (ii) the end of any subsequent period of 12 months for which the
   employee is employed by the employer."

   Section 125B(2) provides that paragraph (1)(b) and subparagraph (1)(c)(ii)
   "do not apply if at the time the employer has employed the employee for the
   period referred to in that paragraph or subparagraph, the employer is a small
   business employer". Whether the Employer is a small business employer is
   [MISSING: small business employer status].

7. CHANGE OF EMPLOYMENT STATUS

   7.1 The Employee's rights about a change to full-time or part-time
   employment are those given by the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) and
   [MISSING: modern award name]. Nothing in this contract limits them.

   Clause 7.1 is based on Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 15A(5) (snapshot
   2026-07-07), which provides that a person who commences employment as a
   casual employee "remains a casual employee of the employer until: (a) the
   employee's employment status is changed to fulltime employment or parttime
   employment under Division 4A of Part 22; or (b) the employee's employment
   status is changed by order of the FWC under section 66MA or 739; or (c) the
   employee's employment status is changed to fulltime employment or parttime
   employment under the terms of a fair work instrument that applies to the
   employee; or (d) the employee accepts an alternative offer of employment
   (other than as a casual employee) by the employer and commences work on that
   basis."

   Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 66AAA (snapshot 2026-07-07) states the object of
   that Division: to establish a framework for dealing with changes to casual
   employment status that "(a) is quick, flexible and informal; and (b)
   addresses the needs of employers and employees; and (c) provides for the
   resolution of disputes to support employee choice about employment status."

VERIFY BEFORE USE
- Whether this draft suits these parties and these facts. The clauses are based
  on the provisions cited. That is not a statement that any clause is
  compliant.
- Award coverage, classification level, base hourly rate and casual loading
  percentage. The corpus retrieval returned Fair Work Act provisions only. It
  returned no modern award text for this engagement.
- Whether the Employer is a small business employer. This changes the timing
  obligations under s 125B(2).
- Whether the engagement is in substance casual. Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
  s 15A(2) assesses that on "the real substance, practical reality and true
  nature of the employment relationship", not on the contract label.
- Whether Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 359B or s 359C is engaged by the
  circumstances of the offer. Section 359B prohibits dismissing or threatening
  to dismiss an employee "in order to engage the individual as a casual employee
  to perform the same, or substantially the same, work". Section 359C prohibits
  a knowingly false statement made "in order to persuade or influence the
  individual to enter into a contract for casual employment".
- Whether the transitional clauses Sch 1 cl 47 and Sch 1 cl 47A apply to this
  employee. Their application depends on facts about employment before
  commencement that were not supplied.
